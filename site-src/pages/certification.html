Method

Certification

“Checked,” “reproduced” and “formally verified” answer different questions. The site reports them separately and only at the scope established by evidence.

01

Source identity

The exact version, byline, DOI or repository identifier, and source locator are established. Publisher and preprint parity remain separate when they have not been compared.

02

Analytic review

The statement and argument are checked under explicit hypotheses. A bounded local result never silently becomes whole-paper certification.

03

Computational reproduction

Finite checks, code, data or figures are reproduced with preserved inputs and negative controls. Numerical agreement is not promoted to proof.

04

Lean formalization

An exact paper-to-theorem map identifies the encoded statement. A reproducible build and axiom audit must show no sorry or unproved new axioms.

05

Editorial integration

The result is explained coherently, with its assumptions and limitations beside it, and with the original authors and any adopted correction properly attributed.

06

External status

Coauthor review, journal action and public correction status are reported independently. An internal finding is never described as an accepted external correction.

What formal verification means here

Lean proves the encoded theorem. It does not by itself prove that the encoding exactly matches the intended paper statement. A public certificate therefore records both the formal declaration and its source-fidelity review.
